The seasonal peak surge

Demand builds through spring, peaks in late May and falls away with the monsoon.

What is happening

Daily energy met peaks on 30 May 2024 at 5,466 MU. June averages 5,080 MU a day, 22% above November. Heat is the likely driver; the file holds no weather data.

Why it matters

The system is sized for the peak: maximum demand hit 250 GW that day. Unmet peak demand stayed small and fell from 349 days to 214.

What could be done

Time-of-day tariffs and demand-response programmes for summer afternoons and evenings.

Unmet peak demand, by month

It was highest in July, a monsoon month, not at the summer peak.

Uneven geographic growth

States and UTs above 10 TWh a year. National average growth is 5.1%.

Jharkhand's +22.4% is positive in all 12 months and is not independently verified (the neighbouring utility DVC fell 3.6%).

What is happening

Five states use 46% of supply. Punjab (+11.1%) and Uttar Pradesh (+11.0%) grew at about twice the national rate; Karnataka shrank (-2.5%).

Why it matters

One national growth assumption misleads; distribution companies in fast-growing states face local capacity limits.

What could be done

Smart-meter rollouts first in high-growth distribution clusters, to map load at feeder level.

Winter demand is peakier

Load factor = daily energy / (that day's maximum demand x 24 h). Lower means a sharper peak.

What is happening

Load factor falls from 94% in Jul-Sep to 83% in Dec-Feb, in both years. The lowest day was 14 Jan 2025 at 80.8%.

Why it matters

Plants sized for the evening peak sit under-used for much of the day, which raises the cost per unit delivered.

What could be done

Smart meters with dynamic pricing that rewards moving winter load out of the evening peak. The cause is a hypothesis; the file has no hourly data.

Can the data be trusted?

Each value was compared with its own 15-day centred median. Outliers were flagged, never overwritten, then tested against neighbouring days and the totals.

Gujarat, 8 Aug 2024

Energy fell 69% to 121.1 MU, yet Western Region evening peak demand stayed normal (56.4 GW against 55.7 GW the day before). This points to a data-communication gap, not a physical supply failure.

Nagaland, 23 Mar 2025

Energy jumped 3.8x to 9.24 MU, and the sum of entities ran 6.4 MU above the India total while the region total showed no spike. This indicates a state-level entry error, not real demand.

Across the window, states and bulk consumers add up to the India total within 0.005% on a typical day, and 99.93% of larger-state days agree with an independent Zenodo series (to 28 Apr 2024).

What this dashboard cannot show
  • Energy met is supply to a state and includes network losses; it says nothing about consumer categories.
  • No hourly or weather data is in these files, so the causes of the seasonal and winter patterns are inferences.
  • The causes of the Gujarat and Nagaland anomalies, and the Jharkhand growth, are unverified.
